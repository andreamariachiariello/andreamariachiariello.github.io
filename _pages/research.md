I use theoretical models and computer simulations to connect physical mechanisms with biological observations.

{% include section-head.html title="3D genome organization" target="genome" %}
<div id="genome" class="section-body prose" markdown="1">

Chromosome folding brings distant genomic regions into contact. My work uses polymer physics and molecular dynamics simulations to investigate how these spatial patterns emerge from molecular interactions. Combining models with chromosome conformation data helps relate physical mechanisms to genome organization across scales.

[Selected publications](/publications/?theme=genome)
</div>

{% include section-head.html title="Chromatin in health and disease" target="disease" %}
<div id="disease" class="section-body prose" markdown="1">

Genetic rearrangements and viral infections can change chromosome organization. I investigate these changes through physical models of chromatin, including work on structural variants and on the organization and dynamics of the host genome during SARS-CoV-2 infection.

[Selected publications](/publications/?theme=disease)
</div>

{% include section-head.html title="Epidemic modelling" target="epidemics" %}
<div id="epidemics" class="section-body prose" markdown="1">

My research also explores simple computational models of epidemic dynamics. Recent work applies a quantum Game of Life approach to West Nile virus outbreak data in Italy. The corresponding preprint is listed separately from peer-reviewed journal articles.

</div>
